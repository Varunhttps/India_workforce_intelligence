# Emerging Skills & Workforce Trends

This dashboard module maps reported workforce problems to a transparent keyword taxonomy of skill clusters and aggregates corroborating evidence.

## What it shows
- Skill cluster, trend label, heuristic signal score, distinct sources, industries, countries and observation weeks.
- Manufacturing-specific skill clusters include industrial automation/robotics, advanced manufacturing/digital operations, maintenance/reliability, quality/process engineering, skilled trades/production, industrial AI/data/analytics, safety/EHS and energy/sustainability.
- Global cross-sector clusters include supply chain/logistics, cybersecurity, cloud/software engineering, project/change management, leadership, communication, customer/sales/service, healthcare/clinical, finance/accounting and learning/reskilling.

## Trend labels
- **Emerging signal**: recent observation, at least two distinct sources, and no more than two observed weeks.
- **Persistent signal**: three or more recorded observation weeks.
- **Cross-industry signal**: evidence from at least two industries without meeting emerging/persistent conditions.
- **Recent signal / Older finding**: temporal fallback labels (Older finding means the evidence is not recent in the current research snapshot; it does not imply the workforce issue is resolved or obsolete).

## Score
The score combines source breadth, industry breadth, country breadth, observation-week breadth and a recent-evidence bonus, capped at 100. It is a sorting aid, not a measure of labor demand or skill scarcity.

## Limitations
Classification is keyword-based and may misclassify context or miss synonyms. Skill labels are clusters, not individual job skills extracted from validated job postings. Trend labels reflect research coverage; sparse history cannot establish growth in real labor-market demand. One source cannot create an Emerging signal.
